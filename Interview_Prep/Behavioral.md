# 🔹 Behavioral Round

Every company runs one, and at Amazon it carries as much weight as coding. You need **8–10 true stories**, each told in about two minutes, that can be bent to fit most questions. Write them once, here, and rehearse them aloud.

## 🔹 The STAR Shape

* **Situation** (2 sentences): where, when, what was at stake.
* **Task** (1 sentence): what *you* were responsible for.
* **Action** (most of the time): what *you* did, in steps, with the reasoning. Say "I", not "we".
* **Result** (numbers if possible): what changed, plus what you learned or would do differently.

Common mistakes: a long setup and a one-line action; "we" throughout, so the interviewer can't tell what you did; no result; a "weakness" that is a disguised strength.

## 🔹 The Questions, Grouped by What They Test

| Theme | Typical questions |
|---|---|
| **Ownership** | Tell me about a project you drove end to end. A time you went beyond your role. |
| **Failure / mistakes** | Your biggest mistake. A bug you shipped. A time a project failed. |
| **Conflict** | A disagreement with a teammate or reviewer. How did it end? |
| **Ambiguity** | A time requirements were unclear. How did you decide what to build? |
| **Learning fast** | A technology you had to learn quickly. How did you go about it? |
| **Trade-offs / judgement** | A decision with no clear right answer. What did you give up? |
| **Quality** | How do you know your code works? A time you raised the quality bar. |
| **Deadlines** | A time you had too much to do. What did you cut? |
| **Customer focus** | A time you changed course because of user feedback. |
| **Why us / why this role** | Prepare 3 reasons specific to the company, not generic praise. |

## 🔹 Where to Find Your Stories

Your own projects already contain most of these; see [Projects.md](Projects.md) for the facts. Prompts worth answering for each project:

* **A decision you reversed.** What evidence changed your mind? (For example: a library or licence that turned out not to fit, and the alternative you shipped.)
* **Something that broke.** How you found the cause, how you fixed it, and what you changed so it can't happen again. Interviewers rate honesty about failure *plus* a concrete prevention step very highly.
* **A number you measured instead of assumed.** Coverage, an evaluation score, a latency figure, and what you did when it was worse than expected.
* **A trade-off you can defend.** What you chose not to build, and why.
* **Learning something hard.** A domain you had to learn from scratch to finish the project.

## 🔹 Questions to Ask Them (pick two)

* What does a successful first six months look like in this role?
* What is the team's biggest technical challenge this year?
* How do code review and on-call work here?
* What did the last person in this role move on to?

## 🔹 Before the Round

* Re-read your resume line by line. Every bullet is a possible "tell me more".
* Rehearse aloud, timed: two minutes per story, then cut to one minute.
* Prepare "tell me about yourself" (60–90 seconds): present → past → why this role.
